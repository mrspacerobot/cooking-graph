# Cook-test pack: three simple recipes

Generated from the graph on 8 October 2026. The recipes below were written by a model from memory and **nobody has ever cooked them**. Every amount, time and temperature is a guess. Cooking them once, carefully, and writing down what happened is the only way a guess becomes something a person can stand behind. You do not need to know the repository.

## How to cook a test

1. **Cook it exactly as written the first time**, weighing and timing with a scale, a measuring jug, spoons and a timer. Write down anything you had to guess at (an oven that runs hot, a different pot, a substitute ingredient).
2. **Do not fix it as you go on the first run.** If a step is unclear or an amount looks wrong, note it and carry on as written so the result tells us about the recipe, not about you.
3. **Then say what you would change** and cook it again that way if you like.
4. Fill in the sheet under each recipe. Short answers are fine. A photo is welcome but not needed.
5. This is a test of taste, texture, amounts and timing. **It is not a safety check.** Nothing here says the food is safe to eat or cooked through, and a recipe that is silent on food safety stays silent.

Where the results go: back to the owner in the conversation, who records them in a dated note under `docs/cook-tests/`. The repository has no `cook-tested` record yet (stage four of `docs/review-record.md` is a design), so a result changes no tier today. It does tell us which guesses were wrong.

4. Drain them on paper, salt them and serve at once.

## Test 2: Turkish bulgur pilaf (`recipe/bulgur-pilavi`)

Coarse bulgur fried with onion and tomato paste, then left under a lid with hot water until every grain is plump and separate. It is served beside meat, stews or yogurt.

Makes: **4 serving**. Equipment named by the recipe: stovetop, saucepan, wooden spoon, chefs knife, cutting board.

| Amount | Ingredient | Was it right? (right / too much / too little / could not tell) |
|---|---|---|
| 300 gram | coarse bulgur | |
| 1 piece | onion | |
| 1 tablespoon | tomato paste | |
| 40 gram | butter | |
| 600 millilitre | water | |
| 1 teaspoon | salt | |

**Steps, with the guessed times and temperatures:**

1. Dice the onion small.
2. Melt the butter in a pan over a gentle heat and soften the onion without colour. Stir in the tomato paste and fry it until the colour deepens. **[guess: 6-8 minute]**
3. Stir the bulgur into the pan until each grain is coated. Pour in the hot water, add the salt and bring to a boil. Cover, turn the heat to its lowest and leave it alone until the water has gone. Take the pan off the heat, lay a cloth under the lid and let it stand, then fluff with a fork. **[guess: 15-20 minute]**
4. Serve hot, heaped in a warm dish.

## Test 3: Sutlac (`recipe/sutlac`)

A creamy rice pudding of rice simmered soft in plenty of milk with sugar, thickened lightly with rice flour and poured into bowls to set as it cools.

Makes: **6 serving**. Equipment named by the recipe: stovetop, saucepan, wooden spoon, colander.

| Amount | Ingredient | Was it right? (right / too much / too little / could not tell) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 gram | rice | |
| 1 litre | milk | |
| 200 millilitre | water | |
| 120 gram | sugar | |
| 2 tablespoon | rice flour | |

**Steps, with the guessed times and temperatures:**

1. Wash the rice in cold water until the rinsing water is no longer cloudy, and drain it.
2. Simmer the rice in the water until it is soft, then pour in the milk and stir in the sugar. Simmer gently, stirring often, until the rice is very soft and the milk has begun to thicken. **[guess: 25-35 minute]**
3. Whisk the rice flour smooth in a little cold milk. Stir it into the pan and simmer for a short while, stirring, until the pudding coats the spoon. **[guess: 3-5 minute]**
4. Pour the pudding into bowls while it is still loose and leave it to cool and set.
