# Introductions and questions for every technique

This round extends the first foundation pass to every existing technique.
It covers 283 introductions and 1,132 questions, with four questions per
technique. The first pass covered 28 foundation techniques; this extension
adds learning content to the other 255. All new content remains `drafted`.

## Content and scope

The introduction explains what the technique does, why it matters for cooking
understanding and an idea to remember. Questions cover purpose, method,
observation and correcting a mistake. Each has a permanent local id, plausible
choices, a correct answer and a direct explanation. Answer positions vary.

Writers use the existing technique's definition, method, cues and mistakes.
Regional names, variants and narrow method scopes stay intact. No original
methods, names, relationships or tiers are changed. No ingredients, dishes,
recipes, safety facts, sources or programs are added. The French foundation
curriculum stays as recorded; all techniques are browsable through the
notebook's Techniques category and cuisine filters.

These are reusable teaching questions, not evaluation probes. No probe files
are inspected or authored by the content writers. An independent model
consistency review does not grant culinary approval or verify safety. Human
review still requires the native review hash and an authorized person.

## Review and doubts

Three writers completed the 255 additional techniques. A different writer
then reviewed every introduction and all 1,020 added questions against the
existing definition, method, cues and mistakes. The review improved plausible
distractors, direct explanations and correction timing. Questions now say
"next batch" or "next attempt" when an action prevents an irreversible defect
rather than repairing an already burnt, split, torn or overworked result.
Every original non-learning field, question id, answer position and tier was
preserved during that review. It is a model consistency check, not a human
culinary approval or evidence of learning effectiveness.

### Doubts for human review

- All source techniques and their new learning text remain drafted. The
  questions can agree with an unverified source without establishing that the
  underlying claim is correct. Review the method and its questions together.
- Shellfish preparation, fish desalting, fish salting, chilling, reheating,
  keeping warm, rillettes storage and marinade reuse have safety-sensitive
  source guidance. The new text does not use smell, appearance, tenderness,
  shell opening or a cold container to certify suitability for consumption.
  Raw tasting and unvalidated holding advice are not repeated as new lessons.
- Pressure-cooker fill, foaming, valve, release and pulse-preparation rules
  need review for the actual appliance and food. New questions stay within
  the described stovetop method and treat pulse softness as texture only.
- Flambe, orange-sauce crepes and dipping sauces have source claims about
  spirits and heating that do not establish alcohol removal or dietary
  suitability. New material does not add those assurances. Flame handling
  still requires authoritative human review.
- Crust colour, bubbles, pan release, soft eggs and sauce texture do not
  establish internal doneness. Frying, grilling, egg sauces and retained-heat
  pasta emulsions remain culinary drafts with separate safety requirements.
- Source claims about cold stock, rice-water methods, salt in boiling water,
  drop lids, repeated octopus dipping and souffle rising need contextual
  culinary review. They are not made universal rules for other techniques.
- Alioli pestle direction, pesto ingredient order, browning in aromatic bases,
  acid or yogurt tenderising, syrup and pastry pairing, mushroom soaking,
  starch behaviour, trussing and sausage pricking have broad or inconsistent
  source claims. Questions avoid the disputed points where possible and keep
  narrow regional and method scopes explicit.
- The original hot-blender handling, tactile syrup tests and room-temperature
  fish guidance need qualified review. Existing source fields were preserved
  rather than silently changing their factual content through a quiz edit.

No factual tier, ingredient assertion or safety verification is promoted by
writing, reviewing or successfully answering these questions.
