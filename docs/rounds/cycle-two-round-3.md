## The reread

Eleven new or changed files were read again: the cards set in aspic, stuff under the skin and make choux pastry, the ingredients gelatin, crozets, Lyon sausage, Roquefort and beef tongue, the preparation choux pastry, and the recipes of the gnocchi and the poularde.

**Six of the eleven were wrong, and were fixed before the check was run again.** The fault in the aliases was then looked for in all the other new ingredients, and found in more of them:

- **The gnocchi recipe had one line of water for two jobs.** It said one amount of water and one of salt, and both the paste and the poaching pan used them. The poaching pan holds far more. It now has a line of water and of salt for the paste and another for the pan.
- **The poularde never skimmed its stock.** The fat of a poached bird sits on top of the stock it makes the sauce from. A skim step now comes before the strain. The sauce was never seasoned either, and now is.
- **Beef tongue was put in the group of braising cuts.** The group is true of it, but five cards work on that group, among them sear, trim and sprinkle with flour, and none is true of a tongue, which is simmered whole and peeled. The tongue is in no group. This is the mistake of round 1 again: reaching for the nearest group, which is wider than the card.
- **Nine names were too wide or said nothing, and were removed.** "Blue cheese" for Roquefort, "rainbow trout" and "river trout" for trout, "aniseed spirit" for pastis and "gizzards" for the duck gizzard each stood for more than one thing. "Salted pork belly" named one cut of salt pork. A farm label on guinea fowl and a Christmas label on capon said nothing, and "flaked almonds" was a form and not a name. A name that stands for several things would flatten a difference.
- **The Lyon sausage was described as both a cooked and a cured sausage.** The entry is now the cooked kind, as the dish asks for, and says so.
- **The gelatin's reason for meat** read as a riddle. It now says that a pork fact counts as meat.

## What is doubtful

- **Every recipe amount and time is from memory.** The choux paste, the poaching times and the amount of truffle were never cooked.
- **Roquefort holds gluten and wheat strictly,** for the bread its mould is traditionally grown on. That is from the drafter's memory and no page was fetched for it. A person has to rule, or find the page.
- **Gelatin holds pork strictly and fish not at all.** Pork is the common kind. Gelatin made from fish or beef exists, and a person must decide whether the kinds need their own entries.
- **Crozets hold wheat, gluten, buckwheat and egg, all strictly,** because both wheat and buckwheat crozets are sold. They are in no group, since a group of wheat pasta would not be true of them.
- **The Lyon sausage is the cooked kind.** The dry-cured saucisson of Lyon is another product and has no entry.
- **The sauce of the poularde** is the stock boiled down with cream, which is a short way to a suprême. A cook of the classic may want a velouté.
- **Capon is a kind of chicken** in the graph. Whether the question commands use that link was not tried.

## What did not fit

- A technique card cannot say that a step is done in the dish it is cooked in. The tongue, the ham in aspic and the gizzard are all cooked first and then finished another way, and the card of the second move has no way to say that the food arrives cooked.
- A preparation that is a dough, such as choux pastry, belongs to no group. Poaching, baking and piping all work on it, and each card names it as a single thing.
- Whether Jambon persillé is a poach, a stocks or a terrine dish is open, as the program has no level for the terrine.
