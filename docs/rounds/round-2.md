## What the model could not express

- **The French sauter.** Poulet sauté chasseur is browned, finished in the same pan and sauced from its fond. A French cook calls all of that sauter. The graph's sauté card describes tossing small pieces in a hot pan, and the recipe names sear, simmer and deglaze. The classical move has no technique of its own yet. This is the kind of flattening the rules warn against.
- **Doneness.** Ten steps end with "check against a trusted guide to doneness" and carry a safety point. The temperatures that define a rare or a medium steak are numbers the graph may not hold until a person has verified them. Tomi's level "Steak to temperature" has no content of its own for that reason.
- **Time that is waiting.** Pears chill for hours and a steak rests for minutes. A step has one time and cannot say whether the cook is busy or free.
- **Time per side.** A steak sears on each side. The step has one time for both.
- **How much of a preparation another recipe takes.** The skate takes a litre and a half of court-bouillon and the court-bouillon recipe yields two. Nothing links the two numbers.
- **Where a by-product's safety facts come from.** Rendered fat has no parts, so nothing says it holds pork. The recipe lists the bacon, and the check keeps by-products out of dishes, so nothing is lost today. An export that shows a step alone would need to know.
- Still open from round 1: a need that holds only for some food, a part that may be left out, the numbers that define a technique, one thing a cook either buys or makes, and the empty `forms` of every ingredient.
