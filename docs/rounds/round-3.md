## What the model could not express

- **A leftover.** Bœuf miroton starts from the beef of yesterday's pot-au-feu. "Boiled beef" became an ingredient. The graph cannot say that one dish is what another leaves.
- **The proportions that define a preparation.** A roux is equal weights. A vinaigrette is one part of acid to three of oil. The parts of a preparation have no amounts, so the proportion lives only in a recipe, or in a sentence without its number.
- **Keeping a sauce warm.** Hollandaise and beurre blanc can be neither boiled nor chilled, and are held warm for a short while. The safety point used is "cooling and keeping", which is the nearest word in the list and not the right one.
- **A brown roux made on its own.** Tomi's level "Brown roux" is practised on bœuf miroton, where the flour browns on the onions. No well-known French dish among the probes starts from a brown roux made by itself. The level may want another dish, or the graph a sauce it does not hold.
- **Sauces that descend from a sauce.** Mornay is béchamel with cheese, béarnaise is hollandaise on a tarragon reduction. None was needed by a dish that was written, so none exists. The model would say it as a preparation that has the mother sauce as a part.
- Still open from earlier rounds: the French sauter, doneness, waiting time, a need that holds only for some food, a part that may be left out, the numbers that define a technique, one thing a cook either buys or makes, and the empty `forms` of every ingredient.
